# CONTEXT — Contekai Footer

**Product.** Contekai — point-of-sale and stock-control software for shops in The Gambia.
Users are shop owners and staff, often on mobile, often on unreliable connections. Trust and
"is this made for us" matter more than corporate polish.

**The job of this footer.** Three things, in order:
1. Convert — get a hesitant owner to start the free trial or open WhatsApp.
2. Reassure — prove the product works offline and that a real human answers.
3. Navigate — quiet access to product pages, login, legal.

**Design thesis.** The footer is a **digital receipt**. Shop owners handle receipts all day;
a footer shaped like one signals "this is genuinely a POS tool," not a generic web template.
The receipt gives structure (line-items, dotted leaders, a "total" that is the CTA); a warm,
logo-shaped terracotta panel gives personality and holds the human/social/WhatsApp trust layer.

**Why not the original.** The current footer stacks nav items as three tall pills and reads as
a leftover mobile menu — huge vertical space, no CTA hierarchy, login mislabelled under
"Company". The receipt structure fixes the density; the warmth panel keeps the friendly voice
the user liked in their reference screenshot.

**Signature element.** The warmth panel's silhouette echoes the Contekai logo mark (a five-point
radial burst). That's the one bold move — everything else stays disciplined and quiet.

**Constraints carried from the project.**
- Deliverable quality is the priority; must not look vibe-coded.
- Encode the project skill standards: Frontend Design, UI/UX Pro Max, Taste, Impeccable, SkillUI.
- Reuse the real logo asset; don't redraw it.
- No fabricated contact data — placeholders get `TODO`s.
- Backend isn't in scope; links can point at existing routes or be left as TODO.
